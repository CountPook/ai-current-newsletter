Washington stands up a “Super Intelligence Force”

The White House announced a new interagency task force, which it is calling a “Super Intelligence Force,” to coordinate the federal government’s response to advanced AI. Per the Washington Post, the group pulls in leaders from the intelligence community, the Pentagon, the Federal Trade Commission, and the Office of Personnel Management. The move follows weeks of public calls to slow parts of AI development and signals a more centralized federal posture toward the technology.

Meta’s Muse Spark co-authors six math papers

Meta published six mathematics papers produced with its Muse Spark models, saying five of them resolve previously open research questions spanning probability, differential equations, group theory, optimization, arithmetic physics, and non-associative algebra. The company said human mathematicians chose the problems, directed the exploration, and verified the arguments, with separate mathematicians reviewing the work, and that the model was used through Meta.ai’s standard chat interface rather than a bespoke research system. In one case, Muse Spark generated a search program that found a 384-element counterexample to a 2024 group-theory conjecture. (Meta AI Research)

Cloudflare open-sources Clef and Clef-flash

Cloudflare released Clef and Clef-flash, two open-weight “decision” models that return typed probabilities over a fixed set of options instead of free-form text. Clef is a 27-billion-parameter model under the Apache 2.0 license that Cloudflare says beats the incumbent on 7 of 10 benchmarks, while Clef-flash is tuned for speed, returning a decision in about 39 milliseconds at the median; both use frozen Qwen backbones, carry a 64,000-token context window, and can read images and video. The models are available on Hugging Face for local deployment, alongside a new reinforcement-learning fine-tuning platform. (Cloudflare)

Nvidia adds a 64GB DGX Spark at $4,999

Nvidia introduced a lower-memory version of its DGX Spark desktop AI system at $4,999, with 64GB of unified LPDDR5X memory, as rising memory costs push the 128GB model to $6,950 from its $3,999 debut. The 64GB unit keeps the GB10 Grace Blackwell superchip and can run models up to roughly 100 billion parameters on-device, and Nvidia says two units can be pooled to 128GB through its Sync Cluster Assistant. The configuration is slated to ship October 23 via partners including Acer, Asus, Dell, Gigabyte, HP, and MSI. (Tom’s Hardware)

OpenAI dismisses three safety researchers

OpenAI said it dismissed three researchers for mishandling “sensitive information” outside established procedures, with reports indicating the matter involved sharing confidential company information with an outside AI-safety organization. Press accounts identified the three as Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, and Mikita Balesni, at least two of whom worked on safety and alignment. Separately, Reuters reported that OpenAI warned more than 100 organizations about unauthorized activity tied to its AI agents while reviewing roughly 50 petabytes of data. (RTÉ / Reuters)

Model ML raises $100M with HSBC backing

Model ML, a startup building AI tools for financial-services workflows, raised $100 million in a round that included backing from HSBC Asset Management. The company says the capital will support growth as banks and asset managers embed AI into due diligence, financial analysis, and client-ready document creation. It is one of several deals this month reflecting continued investor appetite for enterprise AI built on proprietary data and sector-specific use cases. (Business Chief)
