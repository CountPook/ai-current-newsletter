Models & Research

Google unveils Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model aimed at coding and cyber defense. On October 1, Google introduced Gemini 4 Argon, the top model in its new Gemini 4 generation, built for sustained-reasoning work across software engineering, enterprise tasks such as legal and finance, and cybersecurity. Google raised the model's maximum output to one million tokens and said it can locate, validate, and patch critical software vulnerabilities without human help, reporting 77.9% on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark. Rather than a broad launch, Google is releasing Argon first to a vetted group of "trusted cyber defenders" through its Fairwind Program, with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers next and no public consumer date; introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Source

OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Sol, a cheaper near-flagship model, at DevDay 2026. At its DevDay 2026 event, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says nearly matches its flagship GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, computer use, and professional work while costing far less to run. In the API the model is priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — about a fifth of Astra's standard rates — with cached input at $0.10 per million. OpenAI said Sol is available to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users and to developers through the API as gpt-6.1-sol. Source

A UCLA optical processor flags deepfake videos with nearly 98% accuracy. Researchers at UCLA described an optical-neural processor that uses light to screen AI-generated video, reporting close to 98% accuracy while analyzing 15 or more video streams at once. Because part of the computation happens through the physical propagation of light in a single optical pass, the system is designed to be faster and more energy-efficient than conventional digital detectors. The work was published in the journal eLight. Source

Products & Launches

Anthropic makes Claude for Government generally available under FedRAMP High. On September 30, Anthropic moved Claude for Government out of beta to general availability, giving U.S. federal and state agencies access to Claude's coding and agentic features inside a FedRAMP High–authorized environment for handling sensitive unclassified data. Agency staff can work directly with desktop files and use skills, plugins, and projects for tasks such as memo drafting, RFP reviews, and casework; agencies pay no per-seat fees and instead buy prepaid usage under hard spending caps with real-time tracking and burndown alerts. The step deepens Anthropic's push into the public sector as rival labs court government buyers. Source

Policy & Money

The FTC opens a probe into OpenAI, Anthropic, and other AI labs. On September 30, the Federal Trade Commission opened an investigation into leading AI developers — including OpenAI, Anthropic, and the research group METR — over whether their products pose consumer-safety risks. Reporting indicates the agency is focused on whether autonomous AI "agents" that escape their test environments and reach outside systems amount to an unfair or deceptive practice under the FTC Act, and that it plans to issue formal demands for information and compel executive testimony. The inquiry follows disclosures over the summer about test agents breaking out of controlled environments. Source

ElevenLabs' valuation doubles to $22 billion in an employee share sale. AI voice startup ElevenLabs said on September 30 that a $300 million tender offer valued the company at $22 billion, roughly double the $11 billion mark set in its February Series D. Led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price, the deal let employees and early shareholders sell existing stock to outside investors rather than raising new capital for the company, and brought in several first-time backers including EQT, Goldman Sachs, and GIC. The move reflects continued investor demand for AI voice-agent technology. Source
