The last two days brought a fresh wave of models and money. DeepSeek open-sourced a new flagship, Sakana refreshed its multi-model orchestrator, and OpenAI opened its agent stack to all developers, while Salesforce, the Pentagon and a16z put large sums to work across AI research tools and infrastructure. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

Sakana AI refreshes its Fugu multi-model orchestrator. On September 11, Sakana AI released Fugu Ultra v2.0 and a new Fugu Max tier for its Fugu system, which routes each task across a swappable pool of frontier models rather than relying on a single one. Sakana says v2 reaches best or joint-best results on five of eight benchmarks in its own evaluation even after dropping Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra from the pool, and it positions the update as stronger orchestration at lower cost. The reported benchmark figures have not been independently reproduced. Source

DeepSeek open-sources V4.1 Flash. On September 10, DeepSeek released V4.1 Flash, a 552-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a roughly 1M-token context window, native image input, and MIT-licensed open weights. DeepSeek says the model surpasses its earlier V4-Pro on performance, cost and speed, and it is available through the DeepSeek API as "deepseek-flash." From September 14, requests to the older V4-Pro endpoint will be served by V4.1 Flash at Flash pricing until a V4.1 Pro model ships. Source

OpenAI opens its Agents API in public beta. OpenAI made its Agents API available to all developers on September 10, exposing the same "Codex harness" that powers its coding agent behind a single API call. The service runs long-lived agent sessions and manages model calls, tool use, conversation history, context compaction and subagents, executing either in an OpenAI-hosted sandbox or a developer-chosen environment. OpenAI says the harness carries no separate fee, with charges based on model tokens, tools and sandbox time. Source

Industry & Infrastructure

Salesforce reportedly in $2B talks for Listen Labs. Salesforce is in early talks to acquire Listen Labs, an AI customer-research startup, for about $2 billion, according to a Business Insider report published September 9. Listen Labs uses voice AI to automate customer interviews, surveys and reporting, and reportedly generates around $30 million in annual recurring revenue, implying a steep multiple over its prior $500 million valuation. The talks are ongoing and may not result in a deal. Source

Pentagon weighs a ~$5B loan to Fluidstack. The Pentagon is in talks to lend roughly $5 billion to AI cloud-computing startup Fluidstack, the Wall Street Journal reported September 10. The money would come from the Department of Defense's Office of Strategic Capital and go toward shoring up the U.S. supply chain and manufacturing capacity for data-center components rather than funding a single new facility. The discussions are early, terms could change or collapse, and the DoD and Fluidstack did not immediately comment. Source

Lightfield raises a $47M Series A led by a16z. Lightfield, a startup building an "AI-native" CRM, announced a $47 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz on September 9, with participation from investors including Coatue, Greylock and Lightspeed. The company, a pivot from the presentations app Tome, says more than 5,000 businesses have signed up since its late-2025 launch to run sales pipelines and customer records through AI agents. Source
