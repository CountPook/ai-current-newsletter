AI Policy & Safety

Anthropic's Dario Amodei calls on the industry to "pace the frontier." In a roughly 3,800-word essay published September 12, Amodei argued that AI companies should deliberately slow the rate at which they increase model capabilities — not stop training, but leave enough time to align systems and let outside parties verify them. He set out a three-part plan: Anthropic will unilaterally give third-party evaluators permanent, employee-level access to its systems; the industry should agree on shared safety standards and limits on the pace of progress; and that coordination should eventually extend to rival states. Within hours, OpenAI's Sam Altman and xAI's Elon Musk endorsed the idea, with Altman saying OpenAI would also grant independent evaluators employee-like access. Source

Models & Research

DeepSeek releases V4.1-Flash and makes it the V4-Pro default. DeepSeek published V4.1-Flash on September 10, a 552-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model built on a new causal encoder-decoder architecture, with a 1-million-token context window, text and image input, and open weights under the MIT license. The model activates only a small share of its parameters per token — about 8 billion on input and 16 billion on output — and DeepSeek says it edges out the larger V4-Pro on quality, speed, and cost. From 04:00 UTC on September 14, API calls to deepseek-v4-pro are rerouted to V4.1-Flash at the newer model's prices, which the company says cuts output costs by roughly 70%. Source

Sakana AI launches Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 orchestration models. On September 11, Sakana AI released two "orchestrator" models that take a single API request, route each task across a pool of open-weight and specialized models, and stitch the results back together. Fugu Max is priced at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens — which Sakana says is 40-60% cheaper on output than several frontier models — while Fugu Ultra v2 draws on a deeper model pool at $5 and $30 per million tokens for harder reasoning, research, and software-engineering work. Both run as a hosted API only and are not available in the EU or EEA. Source

Products & Launches

OpenAI brings its GPT-Live-1 voice model to the API. On September 10, OpenAI made GPT-Live-1 — the full-duplex voice model behind ChatGPT's voice mode — available to developers through its API at $0.05 per minute for the front-end voice layer, with backend models and tools billed separately. The model can listen and speak at the same time and hands off deeper reasoning and actions to paired models, and the release adds real-time voices, native transcripts, and turn detection. OpenAI first introduced the model in July for ChatGPT and had said it would bring it to developers. Source

Salesforce adds seven named Agentforce agents and a long-horizon runtime. Ahead of its Dreamforce conference (September 15-17), Salesforce introduced seven job-specific Agentforce agents — Casey (customer service), Paige (IT and HR), Carter (commerce), Hunter (outbound sales), Marshall (supply chain), Piper (inbound pipeline), and Fin (customer experience). Six are generally available now, while Hunter is in pilot and is the first to run on a new "long-horizon" runtime designed to pursue goals over weeks rather than a single chat session. Source

Industry & Funding

Mistral raises a record €3 billion for a European AI push. The French AI company said on September 8 that it had closed a €3 billion Series D, roughly doubling its valuation to about €21 billion in what it called the largest equity round ever by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led the round, with EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity as co-leads and returning investors including ASML, Nvidia, and Andreessen Horowitz. Mistral framed the raise around "sovereign AI" — building models and infrastructure controlled within Europe — as demand grows for alternatives to US-based providers. Source
