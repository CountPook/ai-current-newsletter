Over the past 48 hours, OpenAI said one of its models produced a proposed proof of a Millennium Prize math problem, DeepSeek pushed out a faster multimodal model, and the money kept moving — including Europe's largest-ever tech funding round. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

OpenAI says a model produced a proposed proof of a Millennium Prize problem. On September 8, OpenAI said an unreleased model generated a candidate proof of the Navier–Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem, one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million award. OpenAI said the system ran for about 88 hours and coordinated as many as 10,000 agents working in parallel; the result has not yet been peer-reviewed. The announcement drew a dispute over credit after mathematicians Levent Alpöge of Harvard and Tristan Buckmaster of NYU released a related result for the simpler zero-viscosity case on September 7, though OpenAI said it did not see their work before it was made public. DeepSeek begins a beta of V4.1 Flash and plans an official release around September 10. DeepSeek opened a limited beta of V4.1 Flash, a re-architected model that adds native multimodal support and keeps the same API endpoint as its predecessor. The company says the model surpasses its V4 Pro on performance, cost, and speed, and that new "Flash" pricing takes effect on September 10. Access is via an internal test endpoint with a per-account concurrency cap during the trial period. Source

Google DeepMind maps the biological effect of every possible human DNA mutation. Google DeepMind published AI predictions for the likely biological consequences of all roughly 9 billion single-letter changes to human DNA and said it will make the database free to academic researchers. The company said the work is intended to help scientists distinguish mutations that are likely benign from those that may cause disease, speeding up variant interpretation in genetics research. Industry & Business

Mistral raises €3B in a Samsung-led Series D at a €21B+ valuation. On September 8, France's Mistral AI said it raised €3 billion in a Series D funding round at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, which it described as the largest equity fundraising round completed by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led the round, with EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity as co-leads and existing investors including Nvidia and ASML participating. Mistral said the capital will expand its frontier research and compute capacity. Source

Cognition raises $2B at a $48B valuation. AI coding startup Cognition, maker of the Devin agent, raised $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation, according to a September 8 Bloomberg report. Andreessen Horowitz led the round, joined by Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir. The raise continues a run of large funding rounds for AI coding tools. Source

US agencies accuse six Chinese AI firms of large-scale model distillation. On September 8, the NSA, CISA, and FBI issued a joint advisory alleging that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI have run industrial-scale "distillation" against US frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and xAI since late 2024, using fraudulent accounts, bulk subscriptions, and proxy routing to extract training data. The agencies recommended that US providers quietly degrade responses for accounts identified with high confidence rather than block them outright. The named companies did not immediately comment in the reports. Source
