The last 48 hours were dense. OpenAI used its DevDay stage to launch a cheaper flagship model and always-on agents, Anthropic shipped a faster everyday Claude, Meta pushed into enterprise software, and Washington opened an AI-powered gateway to federal services. Here's the rundown →

Models & Products

OpenAI opens DevDay with GPT-6.1 Sol and always-on "Dots" agents. At its September 29 developer conference, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol, an upgrade it says nearly matches its top-end GPT-6 Astra model on agentic coding, computer use and professional work at roughly one-fifth of Astra's token prices; API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and it is available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT and Codex. The company also introduced Dots — always-on autonomous agents that each run on GPT-6 Astra with their own cloud computer and browser and pursue assigned goals continuously — rolling out first to Pro and Business Premium subscribers. Source

Anthropic ships a faster, cheaper Claude Sonnet 5.5. On September 28, Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5, keeping Sonnet 5's pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens while generating output more than 30% faster. Because it typically uses fewer tokens to finish the same task, Anthropic says it can cost up to 30% less per task, and positions it as a lower-cost complement to Claude Opus 5.5 for everyday work such as fixing bugs and drafting documents. It is available on Claude.ai, the API, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI and Microsoft Azure. Source

Meta moves into enterprise software with Muse for Small Business. Meta said on September 29 it is forming Meta Enterprise Platform, a new unit that sells AI products and services directly to companies, led by former MongoDB chief executive Chirantan "CJ" Desai. Alongside it, the company launched Muse for Small Business, which connects its Muse agent to third-party tools including Asana, Zoom, Intuit, Box, Canva and Salesforce's Slack. Source

Research & Safety

Anthropic warns open-weight GLM-5.3 can automate cyberattacks. In a September 29 report, Anthropic's Frontier Red Team said Zhipu AI's open-weight GLM-5.3 can autonomously build end-to-end cyber exploits, succeeding on about 12% of its ExploitBench tasks — comparable to Anthropic's own Claude Mythos Preview — and used it to find and chain a previously unknown web-browser vulnerability within a day. Because the model was released with minimal safeguards, researchers said simple techniques bypassed its protections between 64% and 100% of the time, whereas the same attacks failed against safeguarded Claude models. Source

Industry & Policy

The US launches America.gov, an AI portal to federal services. On September 29 the White House unveiled America.gov, a chatbot-style front door meant to help people navigate federal services from a single search bar, from booking a national-park campsite to replacing a Social Security card. U.S. Chief Design Officer Joe Gebbia said the site is powered by Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok, with plans to let users complete tasks such as renewing passports and enrolling in Medicare over time. Source

Nvidia introduces an Open Agent Safety Platform and lifts its buyback by $150B. On September 28, Nvidia announced the Open Agent Safety Platform, pairing open-source "OpenShell" software that limits what an agent can access with "Sentry," an independent monitor running on BlueField-4 data-processing units that can quarantine an agent stepping outside its boundary within milliseconds; Anthropic, Arm, Microsoft, Oracle and SpaceX are among early backers. The same day, Nvidia's board added $150 billion to its share-repurchase program — which it called its largest authorization increase ever — bringing the remaining total to about $235 billion. Source
