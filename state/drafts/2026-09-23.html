AI moved quickly over the past two days: xAI shipped a larger coding model, OpenAI added two cheaper GPT-6 tiers, Alphabet's Intrinsic open-sourced its robotics platform, a Finnish GPU cloud became Europe's newest AI unicorn, and the UN Security Council took up AI risk in a high-level session. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

xAI ships Grok 4.7, a larger coding-focused model. On September 21, xAI released Grok 4.7, which it describes as its most capable model to date for coding and knowledge work. The company says the model is built on a larger base with a longer reinforcement-learning run — reportedly around 2.1 trillion parameters, up from 1.5 trillion in Grok 4.6 — and reports coding-benchmark gains, including 46.3% on CursorBench 4.0 versus 40.4% for its predecessor. Pricing is unchanged at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, and the model is available in the Grok app, Cursor, Grok Build, and the xAI API. Source

OpenAI adds two cheaper GPT-6 models, Sol and Luna. On September 22, OpenAI expanded its GPT-6 family with Sol and Luna, positioning them as faster, lower-cost alternatives to the flagship GPT-6 Astra. GPT-6 Sol is priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — about half the rate of the GPT-5.6 tier it replaces — while Luna is set at $0.10 and $0.50. OpenAI framed the release primarily as a price-performance step rather than a leap in capability. Source

Products & Launches

Alphabet's Intrinsic open-sources its robotics platform. On September 22, Intrinsic — the robotics software company owned by Alphabet — released Intrinsic Core, opening core parts of its industrial robotics stack under the permissive Apache 2.0 license at the ROSCon 2026 conference in Toronto. The release bundles ROS-compatible components for robot control, motion and grasp planning, machine vision, simulation, and hardware integration, packaged to run on local hardware. Intrinsic said the components are the same ones it uses in its own manufacturing deployments, and framed the move as a step toward wider access to industrial robotics software. Source

Industry & Policy

Finnish AI cloud Verda raises $189M and joins Europe's unicorn ranks. On September 22, Helsinki-based Verda said it raised $189 million in a Series B round led by Emergence Capital, giving the GPU-cloud provider a valuation of at least $1 billion. The company reported an annualized revenue run rate of about $165 million as of July, up from more than $60 million at its April round, and said the funds will support expansion to more than 250 megawatts of capacity across Europe, the UK, the US, and Asia in 2027. Backers included MUFG Innovation Partners, Supermicro, and Finnish state investor Tesi. Source

UN Security Council holds a high-level session on AI risk. On September 23, the UN Security Council — under France's rotating presidency — convened a high-level briefing on artificial intelligence and international security during the UN General Assembly's high-level week. Scheduled briefers included Yoshua Bengio, co-chair of the UN's independent scientific panel on AI; OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, appearing in person; Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, joining remotely; and Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue. Chinese developers DeepSeek and Moonshot were invited to make statements, a rare joint appearance of leading US and Chinese AI firms before the Council. Source
