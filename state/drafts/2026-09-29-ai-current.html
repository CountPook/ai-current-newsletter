Today's biggest moves were about who owns the AI stack and how to keep it in check. AMD struck an $8.2 billion deal for Fei-Fei Li's spatial-intelligence startup, Nvidia opened a platform for reining in autonomous agents, and Anthropic shipped a faster, cheaper Claude — while fresh funding, new agent apps, and a legal challenge to OpenAI rounded out the day. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

Anthropic releases Claude Sonnet 5.5. On September 28, Anthropic introduced Claude Sonnet 5.5, describing it as an upgrade over Sonnet 5 that runs about 30% faster and costs up to 30% less for most work. The release follows Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22, which the company said matches its earlier Fable 5.1 on most tasks while costing roughly 40% less to run than Opus 5. Anthropic framed both updates as efficiency-focused rather than large jumps in capability. Source

Products & Launches

Nvidia launches an Open Agent Safety Platform. On September 28, Nvidia announced an open software platform and reference system design meant to secure AI agents from testing through deployment. It pairs OpenShell, a secure runtime that traces actions and enforces policy as agents run on Nvidia's Vera CPUs, with Sentry, a reference design on BlueField-4 DPUs that monitors agents out-of-band and can quarantine misbehaving ones. Nvidia said more than 100 partners, including Anthropic, Arm, Microsoft, Oracle, and SpaceX, are backing the effort. Source

Manus rolls out Manus 2.0 and a Cue agent app. Also on September 28, Manus released Manus 2.0 and Cue, a standalone app for creating personal agents that can be given their own email address, phone number, and spending wallet. The company said the agents can send messages, take calls and leave summaries, and make payments within a user-set budget, while Manus 2.0 adds a new “Cascade” agent harness, persistent cloud computers, and event-triggered automations. Manus is reported to be raising about $500 million at a target valuation near $4 billion. Source

Industry & Funding

AMD agrees to buy Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 billion. On September 28, AMD said it would acquire World Labs, the spatial-intelligence startup founded by Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock deal valued at about $8.2 billion and expected to close by year-end, subject to regulatory approval. World Labs builds models aimed at understanding physical, three-dimensional environments — technology AMD tied to future robotics and autonomous systems. Li is set to join AMD as executive vice president and chief scientist. Source

Instinct raises $1 billion for a personal AI agent. Instinct, a startup building an agent that completes everyday tasks end-to-end, said on September 28 that it raised a $1 billion Series C from Sequoia, Benchmark, and Coatue at a $10 billion valuation. The company, founded by Noah Shinn, says its still-early-access agent handles jobs such as booking travel, ordering groceries, and canceling subscriptions across apps and phone calls. The round lands roughly a month after a $250 million Series B that valued Instinct at $2.5 billion. Source

Florida's attorney general asks a court to halt new OpenAI models. On September 28, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier filed a motion seeking a temporary injunction that would bar OpenAI from training or releasing new models without independent, third-party safety review, and would restrict minors' access to ChatGPT. The filing cites incidents it attributes to OpenAI systems and requests several categories of relief, including limits on how ChatGPT describes itself. It is a motion, not a ruling — no court has granted the requested relief. Source
