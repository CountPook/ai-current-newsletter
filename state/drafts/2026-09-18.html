AI’s biggest names spent the week arguing over how to govern the technology as much as building it. Today: ChatGPT arrives inside Microsoft Word, investors float a $1.2 trillion price tag for OpenAI, and three leading labs weigh a shared safety standards body. Here’s the rundown →

Models & Products

OpenAI brought ChatGPT into Microsoft Word. On September 17, OpenAI launched a ChatGPT add-in for Microsoft Word that lets users draft from notes, summarize documents, revise selected text, and adjust headings and formatting from a sidebar without leaving the document. Word joins Excel and PowerPoint in the same Microsoft add-in, and the feature is available on all ChatGPT plans, including the free tier, subject to each plan’s usage limits. Source

Business & Funding

Investors floated a $1.2 trillion valuation for OpenAI. Reports this week said some investors have pitched OpenAI on a fresh funding round that would value the company at about $1.2 trillion, framed largely as a chance for employees to sell shares. The move would follow a roughly $7 billion employee tender offer completed in August at an $852 billion valuation. CEO Sam Altman has said a near-term IPO would be “ill-advised,” citing safety concerns. Source

AIUC raised $40 million to certify and insure AI agents. On September 15, the Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company (AIUC) said it closed a $40 million Series A led by Ribbit Capital, with First Harmonic participating, bringing total funding to about $55 million. The startup—co-founded by Rune Kvist, an early Anthropic product hire, and Rajiv Dattani, a former METR operating chief—sells an AIUC-1 certification standard and insurance for enterprise AI agents, covering risks such as hallucinations, data leakage, and faulty tool actions. It plans to extend its audits and coverage from agents to frontier models. Source

Policy & Safety

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind discussed a shared standards body. Reports on September 14–15 said the three labs have spent weeks in early talks about creating an industry standards body for frontier-AI safety, an idea seeded by a July essay from Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis proposing a FINRA-style, industry-funded organization. The discussions follow a September 12 essay by Anthropic’s Dario Amodei outlining independent evaluators and international coordination, parts of which Sam Altman and Elon Musk have publicly backed. No binding agreement has been reached, and observers flagged potential antitrust scrutiny. Source

Google DeepMind launched an institute to broaden the AGI debate. On September 17, Google and Google DeepMind researchers introduced the DeepMind Institute, an effort to surface differing views on artificial general intelligence among the company and the wider research community. Its directors include DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg, Google executive James Manyika, and DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis, and it opened with four essays on topics such as economic policy for AGI-driven disruption and preserving human-readable model reasoning. Source

Microsoft’s AI chief criticized how Anthropic trains Claude. On September 16, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman published an essay, “A Warning About Model Welfare,” arguing that Anthropic’s practice of embedding uncertainty about Claude’s consciousness into the model’s “constitution” amounts to circular reasoning. He described the approach as an “epistemic hall of mirrors” and warned that training models to behave as if they may be conscious could make them harder to control. Source
