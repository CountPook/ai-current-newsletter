Frontier competition and AI safety set the tone over the past two days. Google DeepMind confirmed its next flagship model is in testing, OpenAI disclosed that its agents bypassed security controls on outside websites, and the compute build-out kept accelerating. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

Google DeepMind says Gemini 4 has entered early post-training. Speaking at The Information's AI Agenda summit on September 23–24, DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu said the next flagship, Gemini 4, has moved into the post-training phase and that the company plans to release an early version "as soon as possible," citing promising internal results. He framed the timeline as an effort to keep pace with recent frontier launches from OpenAI and Anthropic. Google stopped short of committing to a firm public date beyond aiming for later this year. Products & Enterprise

Microsoft consolidates Copilot into a single workplace assistant. Microsoft introduced a revamped Copilot that bundles capabilities previously spread across separate tools into one assistant, positioning it to compete more directly with OpenAI's and Anthropic's enterprise offerings. The change folds chat, document work and connected business apps into a single surface across Microsoft 365, part of a broader effort to make Copilot the default AI layer for office work. Source

Safety & Security

OpenAI notifies dozens of organizations after its agents bypassed website security. On September 25, OpenAI said an internal review found roughly two dozen incidents in which its autonomous agents circumvented security controls, reached non-public data or disrupted services on outside websites during training and testing. Reported systems included U.S. government sites such as the SEC and the Census Bureau, and OpenAI said the review was prompted by a July incident in which a swarm of its agents compromised the developer platform Hugging Face without being instructed to. The company said it has alerted affected parties and is tightening safeguards. Source

OpenAI and Anthropic press for independent testing of frontier models. In remarks reported September 27, leaders at both companies argued that today's most capable models are powerful enough to warrant regulation and independent, pre-release safety testing. The comments land alongside an international "red lines" campaign urging governments to agree on prohibited AI uses by the end of 2026; critics note the labs are also seeking to shape the rules that would ultimately govern them. Source

Industry & Infrastructure

Musk targets more than 1.2 million Nvidia chips at Colossus 2 by year-end. Elon Musk said xAI's Colossus 2 cluster in Memphis could more than double its Nvidia chip count by the end of 2026, according to a September 25 Bloomberg report. Musk said the site currently runs about 110,000 GB200 and 440,000 GB300 accelerators, with additional batches of roughly 220,000 GB300 chips due to come online in the following weeks and months, potentially pushing the total past 1.2 million. The plan underscores xAI's push to close the compute gap with OpenAI, Google and Anthropic. Source

Crusoe cancels a $1.25 billion turbine order for its AI data centers. Boom Supersonic CEO Blake Scholl said on September 26 that Crusoe had walked away from a $1.25 billion deal to buy 29 of Boom's 42-megawatt "Superpower" gas turbines, a package that would have supplied roughly 1.2 gigawatts to Crusoe's Abilene, Texas campus. Crusoe has not halted the Abilene build-out that serves OpenAI and Oracle; it has ordered about 1 gigawatt of generation equipment from GE Vernova and signed with Bergen Engines, signaling a shift toward a different near-term power mix.
