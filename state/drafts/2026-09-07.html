AI's dealmaking and efficiency race carried into the long weekend. Nvidia confirmed a nearly $13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, Microsoft and Google shipped cheaper models for speech and weather, AMD unveiled a six-figure desktop AI workstation, and Mira Murati's Thinking Machines lined up a $1 billion raise. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

Microsoft AI releases MAI-Transcribe-2, undercutting rivals on speech. On September 3, Microsoft AI introduced MAI-Transcribe-2, a speech-recognition model it says tops the multilingual FLEURS benchmark across 60 languages with a 5.2% average word error rate. Microsoft claims it runs up to 10x faster than OpenAI's GPT-Transcribe and roughly 5x faster than Google's Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, and priced it at $0.10 per hour of audio — about 72% below the $0.36 it charged for the line's first model five months ago. The model is available on Microsoft Foundry, the MAI Playground, and OpenRouter, and adds diarization, word-level timestamps, and configurable transcription styles. Source

Google DeepMind launches WeatherNext 3 for hourly, high-resolution forecasts. Also on September 3, Google DeepMind and Google Research introduced WeatherNext 3, an AI weather model that generates hourly forecasts at up to 5-kilometer resolution by drawing on live satellite data rather than waiting for datasets that refresh every six hours. Google says day-ahead precipitation forecasts are up to 50% more accurate, with the largest gains in regions where forecasts have historically been less reliable. The model is powering weather features in Google Search, Maps, and the Gemini app, and is available to developers through the Maps Platform Weather API, BigQuery, and Earth Engine. Source

Industry & Business

Nvidia confirms a $12.9 billion deal to buy Hugging Face. Nvidia said on September 3 that it has entered a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face — the platform where more than 18 million developers host over 3 million open models — for about $12.93 billion. Nvidia framed the purchase as a bet on open-source AI and said Hugging Face will stay multi-cloud and multi-accelerator, meaning Nvidia compute will not be required to build on or deploy through the platform. The companies expect the deal to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approval. Source

Thinking Machines Lab is in talks to raise $1 billion at a $40 billion valuation. TechCrunch reported on September 3 that Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab is discussing a roughly $1 billion round led by existing investor Accel, with Nvidia weighing participation. The valuation would exceed $40 billion — well above the $12 billion the year-old startup commanded in its record $2 billion seed round last year, but below the roughly $50 billion it was reported to be seeking. The company, which shipped its first model, Inkling, in July, has an annualized revenue run rate above $100 million. Source

Hardware & Compute

AMD unveils the Threadripper Halo Station, a six-figure desktop AI workstation. At its IFA 2026 keynote on September 4, AMD introduced the Threadripper Halo Station, a liquid-cooled deskside workstation pairing a 96-core Ryzen Threadripper PRO 9995WX with up to four Instinct MI350P accelerators and as much as 2TB of memory. AMD pitched it as able to run models larger than one trillion parameters locally, without a cloud connection, positioning it against Nvidia's DGX Station. Fully configured systems are expected to cost well over $100,000. Source
