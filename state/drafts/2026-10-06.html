Autonomous agents took center stage today as OpenAI opened its DevDay 2026 conference, while Reflection AI pushed out a new open-weight model and both Washington and New York moved to tighten AI oversight. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

Reflection AI debuts Beam, an open-weight model built for lower compute cost. Reflection AI, a two-year-old Brooklyn startup, introduced Beam, its first open-weight model: a sparse mixture-of-experts system with 501 billion total parameters and 23 billion active, pretrained on 23.8 trillion tokens with a one-million-token context window. The company says Beam scores on par with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 on advanced reasoning benchmarks and outperforms leading Western open models while using three to four times less inference compute. Reflection plans to publish the weights under an Apache 2.0 license later in October, along with a technical report, model card, and tooling for running and fine-tuning the model. Source

Products & Platforms

OpenAI centers DevDay 2026 on autonomous agents. At its DevDay 2026 developer conference, OpenAI announced more than 20 updates to ChatGPT, with an emphasis on agents that can take on ongoing work. New features include Dots, persistent agents that handle continuing tasks, and ChatGPT Space, a shared workspace where teams and agents operate with common context. OpenAI also added computer use to its Agents API, letting developers build agents that operate software directly, and expanded the plugin system with sidebar experiences, interactive conversation panels, and custom file viewers. The company reiterated that GPT-6.1 Sol, its coding- and tool-focused model, is priced at about one-fifth of flagship GPT-6 Astra's token rates. Source

Policy & Governance

Trump creates a federal "Super Intelligence Force." President Trump announced a White House task force, which he called the "Super Intelligence Force," to coordinate federal AI policy. It will be led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, OPM Director Scott Kupor, and Emil Michael, and is charged with producing a report within 120 days outlining AI's risks and opportunities and the federal government's role. The announcement was light on specifics about the group's authority over AI development. Source

Top AI labs testify before the New York City Council. Senior leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta gave sworn testimony before the New York City Council on Monday about the risks AI poses to the public—the first such appearance by major AI companies before a legislative body. The four firms agreed to attend only after council speaker Julie Menin authorized subpoenas; the hearing centered on her bill that would bar selling or deploying an AI model in the city unless an outside validator has reviewed it and a person can shut it down. The council separately subpoenaed SpaceX's AI unit and said it may seek court enforcement if the company does not comply. Source

Industry & Business

RobCo crosses a $1 billion valuation on a physical-AI wave. Industrial-automation startup RobCo passed a $1 billion valuation through a secondary share sale, roughly doubling its worth in nine months as investors keep backing physical AI. The company, which builds AI-driven robotics for manufacturers, says it has deployed more than 1,000 robots to industrial customers, including BMW. The milestone caps a first half of 2026 in which robotics and physical-AI startups raised a reported $33.4 billion, according to PitchBook data. Source
