Models & Research

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna at lower prices. On September 22, OpenAI added two models to its GPT-6 line: Sol, positioned for everyday professional work, and Luna, a cheaper model tuned for fast, high-volume responses. The company priced Sol at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and Luna at $0.10 and $0.50 — roughly half the cost of the comparable 5.6-series models — and said both carry a context window of about 1.05 million tokens. OpenAI reported that Sol makes about half as many mistakes as its predecessor on an internal factuality evaluation and is rolling the models out across ChatGPT and its API. Source

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5.5. Also on September 22, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its 5.5 family, priced at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens — about 20% below Opus 5 — with cache reads cut roughly 60% to $0.20 per million tokens. The company said the model runs about 40% cheaper than Opus 5 on typical workloads with output speeds more than 30% faster, and is available through AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure. Anthropic said Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 versions would follow in the coming weeks. Source

MIT gives an insect-scale flying robot a new AI controller. MIT engineers described an AI-based control system that lets their sub-gram flapping-wing robot perform aggressive aerial maneuvers, reporting roughly a 450% increase in speed and about 250% higher acceleration over their previous results. In tests the robot completed 10 consecutive somersaults in 11 seconds and held its path against wind disturbances, using a two-part controller designed to balance performance against limited onboard computing. The researchers suggested such robots could eventually search collapsed structures and other spaces conventional drones cannot reach. Source

Products & Launches

Meta centers Connect 2026 on its Muse AI agent and new hardware. At its September 23 Connect event, Meta made its Muse personal assistant the centerpiece of a hardware lineup that included $1,299.99 Meta VR Glasses (due spring 2027), a palm-sized Muse Charm companion device slated to go on sale in December, and an expanded glasses range the company says will exceed 100 styles by year-end. Meta also previewed Muse Realtime Avatar, a model that lets users video-chat with an animated version of their agent, and deeper Muse integration across its smart glasses. The announcements underscore Meta's push to move AI features onto wearable devices. Source

Industry & Funding

Google DeepMind says Gemini 4 is nearing release. On September 24, DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu said the next flagship, Gemini 4, is in an early post-training phase, with the company aiming for an initial release before the end of the year. The comments position Google's next model against OpenAI's new GPT-6 line and Anthropic's freshly shipped Opus 5.5. DeepMind did not commit to a firm launch date or publish benchmarks. Source

PicoJool raises $27.5M for optical interconnects aimed at AI clusters. The semiconductor-laser startup, backed by former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger, said on September 24 it closed a $27.5 million Series A led by Socratic Partners with participation from Hudson River Trading, bringing total funding to $39.5 million. PicoJool is developing laser-based optical links intended to move data faster and more efficiently between chips in large AI training and inference clusters. The round adds to a steady flow of capital into AI data-center infrastructure. Source
