Frontier releases kept coming this week. DeepSeek open-sourced V4.1-Flash, a faster multimodal model with public weights; Apple used its September event to introduce a foldable iPhone and lean harder into on-device AI; and investors poured fresh capital into AI coding, model testing, and agent-native software. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

DeepSeek open-sources V4.1-Flash, a faster multimodal model. On September 10, DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, a mixture-of-experts model it says surpasses its earlier V4-Pro on performance, cost, and speed. The company published open weights under an MIT license on Hugging Face and describes the model as having roughly 552 billion total parameters — activating about 16 billion per token during decoding — a 1 million-token context window, and native image understanding built into the base model. It also supports tool calls, JSON output, and endpoints compatible with rival APIs. Source

Products & Platforms

Apple introduces a foldable iPhone and leans into on-device AI. At its September 9 event — the first led by new CEO John Ternus — Apple unveiled its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro. Both run a new A20 Pro chip that Apple says adds neural accelerators for low-latency on-device AI and a roughly 20% faster CPU. Apple also put its revamped Siri at the center of the pitch, saying the assistant now works across more than 300,000 apps. Source

Meta acquires Sweden's Stilla.ai to power business-messaging agents. Meta agreed to buy Stilla, a Stockholm-based startup founded in 2024 that builds AI agents able to work across an organization's tools with shared context and permissions, according to reports on September 9. Meta said the team and technology will support its Business Agent, which automates customer conversations and transactions across WhatsApp, Messenger, and Instagram and is used by more than one million businesses. Stilla had raised about $5 million in pre-seed funding. Source

Funding & Deals

Cognition raises $2B at a $48B valuation. AI coding startup Cognition, maker of the autonomous software-engineering agent Devin, said on September 8 it raised $2 billion in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz, Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir. The financing roughly doubles the $26 billion valuation it set in May and comes as run-rate revenue climbs toward $900 million. Source

Alibaba to lead a $300M round in model-testing startup UniPat AI. Alibaba is set to lead a $300 million investment in UniPat AI, a benchmarking and stress-testing startup, at a $2.5 billion valuation, according to a September 10 Bloomberg report. Tencent and prior backer HSG (formerly Sequoia China) are also participating. UniPat, founded by a former Alibaba lab intern, evaluates models before they run automated financial workflows or access live consumer data. Source

Lightfield raises $47M for an agent-native CRM. Lightfield said on September 9 it raised a $47 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz to build what it calls a CRM structured as a "world model" of a business, ingesting emails, calls, and meetings instead of relying on manual data entry. The company, which pivoted from presentation startup Tome, reported more than 5,000 customers since launching publicly in late 2025. Source
