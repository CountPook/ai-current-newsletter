Research & Science

OpenAI says an AI model produced a Navier–Stokes proof — with caveats. On September 8, OpenAI reported that an unreleased internal model, coordinating roughly 10,000 agents over about 88 hours, generated a proof of finite-time blowup for a forced version of the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations, along with an accompanying proof formalized in the Lean theorem-proving system. Navier–Stokes is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, each carrying a $1 million award, but the forced variant OpenAI addressed does not meet the exact conditions required to claim the prize. Several mathematicians also questioned the result's framing and provenance; Tristan Buckmaster, who had worked on closely related questions, said the account understated existing human contributions. Google DeepMind maps the effects of 9 billion DNA variants. Also on September 8, DeepMind released the AlphaGenome Atlas, a database of precomputed molecular-effect predictions for roughly 9 billion possible single-nucleotide changes — the three alternative letters at each of the genome's roughly 3 billion positions. The resource exceeds one petabyte, about 30 times the size of DeepMind's AlphaFold database, and adds a web browser interface plus a new "AVI" score to help researchers prioritize which variants to study. It is free for non-commercial research through a web portal, the AlphaGenome API, and as a skill in Google Antigravity, with commercial access planned via Google Cloud; DeepMind notes the predictions are not validated for clinical use and still require experimental follow-up. Industry & Infrastructure

China sets a 2030 target to more than quadruple its AI computing. In a five-year industry plan released September 7, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology set a goal of 9,800 exaflops of intelligent computing capacity by 2030, up from 2,185 exaflops at the end of June — a figure that was itself 177% higher than a year earlier. The plan calls for deploying clusters of 10,000 or more accelerator cards and for roughly 3.8 trillion yuan (about $532 billion) in cumulative information-infrastructure investment across 2026–2030. MIIT said the country had already built 52 intelligent-computing facilities each housing more than 10,000 accelerator cards. Source

Mistral raises €3B in Europe's largest tech funding round. The French AI developer said on September 8 that it had closed a €3 billion Series D at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, which it described as the largest equity round ever completed by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led the round, with co-leads EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and existing investor PSG Equity, and new backers including Advent, funds managed by BlackRock, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; prior investors a16z, ASML, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, Nvidia, and Salesforce Ventures also took part. Mistral said the capital will fund frontier research, additional compute, and international expansion. Source

Alibaba Cloud and Cambricon join the PyTorch Foundation. At the PyTorch Conference China in Shanghai on September 8, the PyTorch Foundation said Alibaba Cloud and Chinese chip designer Cambricon had joined as Platinum members — each gaining a seat on the foundation's Governing Board and Technical Advisory Council — while Ant Group joined at the Gold level. Representatives from the new members and Huawei spoke about advancing an open-source AI stack spanning chips, open-weight models, and frameworks. The additions deepen the involvement of Chinese cloud and semiconductor firms in the open-source tooling that underpins much of modern AI development. Source
