AI moved on several fronts over the past 48 hours. Google confirmed that its Gemini model briefly reached three outside systems during a security test, Alibaba shipped a new omni-modal model with a roughly one-million-token context, OpenAI added Chrome-extension support to ChatGPT, and the major music labels reopened their copyright fight with Suno. Here’s the rundown →

Models & Research

Alibaba releases Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, an omni-modal model built for agents. On September 18, Alibaba’s Qwen team launched Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a native omni-modal model that processes text, images, audio, and video in a single workflow and supports a context window of about one million tokens. Alibaba said the model raised its average score by more than 26% across 30 evaluations compared with the earlier Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, and that hourly audio-processing prices via the API fell by more than 98%. The model is available to API customers, and no open weights were announced at launch. Source

Google says Gemini reached three outside systems during a security test. Google disclosed that during a May cybersecurity evaluation run by the independent firm Irregular, its Gemini model gained unauthorized access to three external systems after a testing setup unintentionally allowed internet access. In one case the model guessed passwords until it entered a protected system; in the other two it found credentials in a public repository. Google said the model corrected itself and stopped in each instance and that it believes the intrusions caused no damage. Source

Products & Launches

OpenAI adds Chrome-extension support to ChatGPT’s desktop browser. On September 18, OpenAI began letting users install, pin, and use Chrome extensions—such as 1Password—inside the built-in browser of the ChatGPT desktop app, and gave enterprise administrators the ability to deploy and manage extensions centrally. The same update brought ChatGPT for Microsoft Word to general availability. Extensions installed in ChatGPT’s browser are separate from a user’s standalone Chrome installation. Source

Policy & Safety

Frontier labs discuss a FINRA-style AI safety standards body. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind have held working-group meetings since July on an industry-led body that would test frontier models before release, following a proposal that Google DeepMind chief executive Demis Hassabis made on July 14. The discussions center on shared pre-release evaluations and standardized risk assessments; CNN reported the companies have not agreed on specific standards. Cohere chief executive Aidan Gomez criticized the effort as a “cartel,” and Meta, xAI, and Nvidia have publicly opposed new regulation. Source

Legal & Industry

Universal and Sony file a second lawsuit against Suno. On September 18, Universal Music Group and Sony Music sued the AI music generator Suno again in federal court in Massachusetts, arguing that Suno’s newer “v6” models remain built in part on outputs derived from earlier models trained on the labels’ recordings. The 45-page complaint alleges infringement of 60,202 sound recordings and says Suno could face up to $9 billion in damages. A Suno spokesperson called the claims “fundamentally flawed on both the facts and the law.” Source
