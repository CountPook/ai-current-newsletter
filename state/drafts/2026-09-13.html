The past day pushed cheaper, more capable models further into the open: DeepSeek shipped an inexpensive open-weight model, Sakana launched a system that routes work across other models, and OpenAI opened a full-duplex voice model to developers. Off the model page, Anthropic detailed how it says it caught misuse of Claude, and Washington sharpened its focus on frontier-AI safety. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

DeepSeek releases V4.1-Flash, an inexpensive open-weight model. DeepSeek published V4.1-Flash, a 552-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates roughly 8 billion parameters per input token and 16 billion on output, with a one-million-token context window, native image input, and MIT-licensed weights for self-hosting. The company lists off-peak API pricing at $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens, roughly doubling at peak hours, and reports a 74.2 score on the DeepSWE v1.1 software-engineering benchmark. DeepSeek says the model was trained from scratch on 45 trillion tokens of mixed text and images. Source

Sakana AI launches Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2.0 for multi-model orchestration. Sakana AI released two systems built to route each request across a pool of open-weight and specialized models rather than answer directly, stitching the results into one response behind a single API. The company says Fugu Ultra v2.0 carries a one-million-token context window and posts top or joint-top scores on five of its eight headline agentic benchmarks, priced at $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output tokens. Sakana notes that frontier models such as Anthropic's Fable 5 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra are not part of Fugu's routing pool. Source

Products & Infrastructure

OpenAI opens GPT-Live-1, a full-duplex voice model, in its API. OpenAI released GPT-Live-1, a speech model that can listen and speak at the same time and is designed to handle pauses, interruptions, and mid-conversation changes in direction. It adds 12 new voices, turn detection, and native transcripts, and OpenAI lists the front-end voice layer at about $0.05 per minute. The company reports that, paired with GPT-6 Astra at medium reasoning effort, the system completed 83.6% of tasks in its Tau3 customer-support benchmark on the first attempt, compared with 45.7% for its earlier GPT-Realtime-2.1. Safety & Policy

Anthropic publishes a detailed report on disrupted Claude misuse. Anthropic released a threat-intelligence report covering misuse it says it detected and disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026 across seven categories, including cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance, fraud, biological misuse, conventional-weapons development, and model distillation. The company says the cases largely involved its Claude Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus models used as an execution layer inside multi-agent frameworks, and warns that newer Claude versions can no longer be assumed to fall safely below the threshold for meaningful bioweapons assistance. Anthropic says it disrupted every operation described in the report. Source

Washington sharpens its focus on frontier-AI safety. Two AI researchers left Anthropic and Google this week citing safety concerns, according to NBC News, as lawmakers pressed for closer oversight of the most capable systems. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said the company is open to slowing development as safety questions grow, while chief scientist Jakub Pachocki said no lab has yet solved alignment and monitoring well enough to keep scaling at maximum speed indefinitely. Senators Ted Cruz, John Thune, and Amy Klobuchar are reported to be working on legislation addressing catastrophic AI risks. Source
