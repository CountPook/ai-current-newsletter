The last few days brought a run of frontier releases and fresh capital. World Labs showed a model that builds explorable 3D worlds; Microsoft and Multiverse Computing shipped new speech and reasoning models; xAI and CrowdStrike pushed AI deeper into enterprise work and security; and Cognition lined up a round that would roughly double its valuation. Here's the rundown →

Models & Research

World Labs debuts Atlas, an “omni” world model. On September 1, World Labs — the spatial-intelligence startup co-founded by Fei-Fei Li — unveiled Atlas, which it describes as an “omni” world model that generates explorable 3D environments from a single image or text prompt. The company says Atlas produces camera-controllable video up to 1440p and roughly one minute long, and that human raters preferred its output to competing video models in 75–94% of comparisons. Atlas is not yet a product: it is entering early access with selected partners through a request form, with no public pricing or API announced. Source

Microsoft releases MAI-Transcribe-2, a low-cost speech model. On September 3, Microsoft AI released MAI-Transcribe-2, a speech-recognition model it says ranks first on the FLEURS benchmark across 60 languages, with an average word error rate of 5.2%. The model adds speaker diarization, word-level timestamps and keyword biasing, and is available through Azure’s Microsoft Foundry Speech service. Microsoft is pricing it at $0.10 per hour of audio through the end of 2026 and has not said what it will cost afterward. Source

Multiverse Computing launches Quasar 438B. On September 2, Spain’s Multiverse Computing introduced Quasar 438B, a 438-billion-parameter reasoning model aimed at enterprise agents and coding and offered through its CompactifAI API. The company says Quasar scored 43 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (v4.1.1), which it calls the highest of any European model evaluated, ahead of Mistral Medium 3.5 and Nvidia’s Nemotron 3 Ultra. The model supports English and Spanish and pairs long-context reasoning with agentic coding and terminal tasks. Source

Products & Security

xAI opens Grok Bot to enterprises. xAI has opened Grok Bot, its autonomous-agent platform, to enterprises, adding network, team-management and audit controls for administrators. Each bot runs on its own cloud machine with access to a browser, files and a terminal, can carry out multi-step tasks such as research and outreach, and can hand work to other bots; xAI says the workers keep running after a user logs off. The company is offering Grok and Cursor Enterprise customers a two-week free trial and lists Legora, Supermicro and ServiceTitan among early adopters. Source

CrowdStrike launches SafeMind, built with Nvidia. At its Fal.Con 2026 conference, CrowdStrike introduced SafeMind, an agentic cybersecurity system built on Nvidia Nemotron models. It pairs an offensive model, Red Tempest — trained partly on 15 years of CrowdStrike incident-response data — with a defensive model, Blue Solano; the two run in a closed loop against a digital twin of a customer’s environment until no attack paths remain. CrowdStrike says SafeMind runs inside its Falcon platform and claims a 29% higher detection rate and roughly 6x faster remediation than leading commercial models. Source

Funding & Business

Cognition reportedly in talks to raise about $1B at a ~$47B valuation. Cognition, the startup behind the Devin coding agent, is in talks to raise around $1 billion in a round that would value it at roughly $47 billion, Bloomberg reported on September 2. That would be nearly double the $26 billion valuation it reached in May; the company is said to be fielding close to $10 billion in investor interest and to be generating more than $900 million in annualized revenue. The round has not closed and terms could still change. Source
