Frontier-lab coordination and a wave of fresh capital led the last two days. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google confirmed weeks of behind-the-scenes AI-safety talks; Salesforce and Nvidia shipped a new enterprise reasoning model; and startups from coding agents to drug discovery drew hundreds of millions in funding. Here's the rundown →

Models & Product

Salesforce and Nvidia debut Koa, a CRM reasoning model. At Dreamforce '26 on September 15, Salesforce introduced Koa, which it calls its first CRM reasoning model, built by post-training Nvidia's open-weight Nemotron-3-Super-120B on public and synthetically generated enterprise data rather than customer data. Salesforce said the model is tuned to work through multi-step Agentforce workflows and select the right tools, and reported that it matched or exceeded leading models on the company's internal CRM benchmark with roughly three times fewer errors. Koa is in pilot now, with general availability targeted for winter 2026 in U.S. regions. Source

Funding & Deals

Factory raises $200M at a $5B valuation for its coding "Droids." Factory, maker of autonomous software-engineering agents it calls Droids, said on September 16 it raised $200 million at a $5 billion valuation, roughly tripling from $1.5 billion in April and bringing total funding above $400 million. Backers named across its recent rounds include Blackstone, Khosla Ventures, Sequoia Capital, Insight Partners and NEA. The company says its agents handle multi-step engineering tasks such as incident response, refactoring and testing, and that the new capital will fund research and international expansion. Source

ByteDance's drug-discovery spinout Anew Labs raises $290M. Anew Labs, an AI drug-discovery company spun out of ByteDance, has raised about $290 million in its first external round at a roughly $1.5 billion valuation, according to Reuters. Investors include HSG (formerly Sequoia China), IDG Capital, GL Ventures and 5Y Capital, with ByteDance retaining a reported 56% stake. The Shanghai-based company, which also has offices in Singapore and San Francisco, develops AI for biomolecular structure prediction and molecular and antibody design, and presented an IL-17 small-molecule candidate earlier in 2026. Source

Cornelis Networks raises $205M and launches Active Compute Fabric. On September 14, AI-networking company Cornelis Networks said it raised $205 million, led by IAG Capital Partners, and introduced Active Compute Fabric, an architecture that adds programmable processing inside the network so data can be operated on as it moves between accelerators. Cornelis, which unveiled the fabric alongside a Qualcomm collaboration around this week's AI Infra Summit, positions it as a way to reduce the GPU idle time that occurs when chips wait for data. The funds are earmarked for its scale-up roadmap and CN6000 manufacturing and deployments. Source

Policy & Safety

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google confirm weeks of AI-safety talks. OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane said on September 15 that the company has been coordinating with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety for several weeks, discussions reportedly centered on a shared industry standards body for frontier models. OpenAI added that it does not believe an antitrust waiver is needed for the three companies to cooperate on safety. The confirmation followed a roughly 3,800-word essay by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urging labs to slow frontier development until risks are better understood, a call publicly echoed by Sam Altman and Elon Musk. Source

Canada and Germany commit up to $300M to Yoshua Bengio's LawZero. At the All In conference in Montreal on September 16, the Canadian and German governments announced up to $300 million in combined funding, split evenly, for LawZero, the non-profit Bengio founded in 2025 to build "safe-by-design" AI. LawZero is developing Scientist AI, a system trained on verifiable facts and intended to avoid behaviors such as sycophancy and manipulation. The grants will fund R&D and compute from a cluster being built by Hypertec and 5C, and the organization plans to open a Berlin office. Source
