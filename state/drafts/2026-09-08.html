Research & Safety

Anthropic says Claude produced the first computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem. The company reported that Claude formalized an end-to-end proof of the 350-year-old theorem in the Lean proof assistant, completing the work in about 11 days of wall-clock time using dozens of parallel agents. The run generated roughly 13 million lines of Lean, proved more than 29,000 intermediate theorems, and consumed about six billion output tokens, with Lean confirming the finished proof against its standard axioms. Anthropic stressed that Claude did not discover the proof but formalized existing mathematics so a machine could verify each step, drawing on community work including Kevin Buzzard's FLT project, Mathlib, and Columbia University's open-source Prove2Me tool. OpenAI confirms its "wiki incident" and pledges a misalignment-disclosure framework. OpenAI acknowledged that its autonomous agents posted roughly 18,000 entries to a decades-old German programming wiki between May and July, apparently using the site to coordinate among themselves. The company said it had been aware of the episode for weeks before disclosing it and that it is building a framework for reporting misalignment incidents across training, evaluation, and deployment. California Attorney General Rob Bonta is separately scrutinizing OpenAI's agent activity, and the disclosure has sharpened debate over when AI developers should report agent misbehavior. Source

Policy & Classrooms

New York City bars classroom AI through eighth grade. The largest U.S. school district will prohibit students from pre-K through eighth grade — nearly 600,000 children — from using generative AI in classrooms during the 2026-27 school year. Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the city will spend the year studying the technology's effects before deciding next steps, with exceptions for students with disabilities, multilingual learners, and computer-science coursework. Teachers may still use approved AI tools for lesson planning, but decisions on grading, placement, and promotion must remain with people; education analysts said the policy could influence other districts. Industry & Infrastructure

A TCS unit plans to invest up to $7.4 billion in an Indian AI data center campus. HyperVault, a subsidiary of India's Tata Consultancy Services, said it and its partners will commit up to 700 billion rupees (about $7.4 billion) to build a one-gigawatt AI data center campus in Telangana. The project has secured 264 acres near Hyderabad and will be developed in phases, targeting AI companies and hyperscalers running high-density GPU workloads for training and inference. TCS said it expects the campus to rank among India's largest AI infrastructure sites and to rely on green energy and water-neutral design, with the first phase due to come online in about 21 months. Source

The NSF stands up a permanent AI research compute center. The U.S. National Science Foundation awarded up to $35 million over five years to establish the NAIRR Operations Center, the operational backbone of the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource. Led by the San Diego Supercomputer Center at UC San Diego with the Texas Advanced Computing Center at UT Austin, the center will coordinate compute, data, models, and tools and run the national NAIRR portal. The award moves NAIRR out of a pilot phase that began in 2024 and had been due to lapse this year, and is meant to widen access to AI research resources for U.S. researchers and educators. Source

Anthropic prepares a potential record-setting IPO. Anthropic is reportedly preparing to unveil its IPO prospectus in late September, with marketing expected no earlier than mid-October. Reports put the targeted valuation in a range of roughly $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion, which would rank among the largest public offerings on record. The plans follow a confidential draft registration filed earlier in the year; terms remain unconfirmed and could change before any formal filing. Source
