A busy stretch for AI: Apple shipped its long-promised Siri overhaul, two labs released cheaper models, and research and corporate-finance news filled out the day. Here's the rundown →

Products & Launches

Apple releases iOS 27 with a rebuilt, AI-powered Siri. Apple began rolling out iOS 27 — along with iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, and visionOS 27 — on September 14, headlined by a new version of Siri that draws on a user's on-device context, such as messages, email, and photos, and can take more actions across apps. The assistant ships as an opt-in English beta, with French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish support planned for October; Apple's AI features require an iPhone 15 Pro or newer. Apple described the release as including more than 250 changes across its operating systems. Source

Model Releases

Sakana AI ships Fugu Ultra v2 and a lower-cost Fugu Max. Tokyo-based Sakana AI released two models on September 11 built around learned orchestration — routing tasks across a pool of specialized models, and calling copies of themselves, rather than relying on a single large network. Sakana reports that Fugu Ultra v2 tied or led on five of the eight benchmarks it published, including a 74.3 score on the DeepSWE software-repair test, and offers a one-million-token context through an OpenAI-compatible API priced at $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output. Source

DeepSeek releases V4.1-Flash, then walks back a plan to retire V4-Pro. DeepSeek published V4.1-Flash on September 10, a mixture-of-experts model with a 552-billion-parameter backbone that activates roughly 16 billion parameters to generate output, ships under an MIT license, and supports a one-million-token context; the company says it outperforms its flagship V4-Pro on quality, cost, and speed. DeepSeek initially said all V4-Pro API traffic would route to V4.1-Flash at the cheaper Flash rates starting September 14, but later said it would keep V4-Pro available with unchanged pricing, citing user demand. V4.1-Flash is priced at $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output at peak. Source

Research

Google DeepMind releases AlphaGenome Atlas. On September 8, DeepMind published a free database of AI-generated predictions for the molecular effects of all roughly nine billion possible single-letter DNA changes in the human genome. The roughly one-petabyte resource introduces an AlphaGenome Variant Impact score that ranks mutations by their predicted biological effect to help researchers prioritize which to study; DeepMind says the atlas is intended for research rather than clinical diagnosis. Source

OpenAI claims a Navier-Stokes result, prompting a credit dispute. Also on September 8, OpenAI said it had produced a solution related to the Navier-Stokes equations — a long-standing problem in mathematics — using a large collection of AI agents running an internal model, along with a formalization in the Lean proof assistant, and said it would not claim the associated $1 million Clay Millennium Prize. The announcement was quickly contested: mathematician Tristan Buckmaster said related Euler-equation work he and a collaborator had done was shared with OpenAI shortly beforehand, a characterization OpenAI disputed, citing differences in method. The claim had not been peer-reviewed at the time of the announcement. Source

Industry & Business

Altman says OpenAI will not pursue an IPO in 2026. In an interview with Fortune, OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman said the company would not go public this year, calling it an "ill-advised moment" and pointing to work still needed on AI safety and alignment; he indicated any listing would not come before 2027. Earlier reports had discussed a potential offering at around a $1 trillion valuation. Source
