A busy day across models, safety and money. In this issue: xAI's more accurate speech-to-text model, a new probabilistic "decision" model from a ChatGPT co-creator, Alibaba's transparent image model, OpenAI's frontier-safety proposals, Anthropic on how much of its own R&D Claude now runs, Washington's plan for an "AI Force," and Nscale's IPO filing. Here's what happened →

Models & Products

xAI ships Grok Voice Transcribe 2.0. xAI released a new speech-to-text model it says is roughly twice as accurate as the prior version while keeping the same $0.10-per-hour batch price. It reached the company's API on September 17 and opened publicly the next day, and xAI says it ranks first for accuracy among the streaming models tracked on the public Artificial Analysis leaderboard. The update adds multilingual transcription, word-level timestamps, speaker diarization and multichannel support.

Source: xAI · Unite.AI

A ChatGPT co-creator launches Jev, a probabilistic "decision" model. TypeSafe AI, founded by ChatGPT co-creator Diego Almeida, unveiled Jev, a foundation model that emits decisions as calibrated probabilities rather than generated text. Trained with reinforcement learning on labeled decisions, the company aims it at classification and routing tasks and claims it can run about 100 times faster and cheaper than a conventional large language model on those workloads. It is the first release in what TypeSafe calls its "System One" line.

Source: TechCrunch

Alibaba releases Qwen-Image-2.1 with native transparency. Alibaba shipped a 7-billion-parameter image model that can generate native transparent (RGBA) output at up to 2048×2048 and accept as many as ten reference images. With this version, Alibaba moved the license to research-only weights, requiring a separate grant for commercial use.

Source: AlphaSignal

Research & Safety

OpenAI proposes shared standards for frontier AI safety. OpenAI published a set of proposals for safety and security in frontier AI development, centered on keeping alignment research in step with fast-improving systems and on the risks of recursive self-improvement (RSI). The company called for international cooperation on common frontier standards and said the effort should build on existing government AI safety institutes rather than start over.

Source: CNBC

Anthropic says Claude now leads about a quarter of its R&D. Anthropic said its Claude models lead roughly 25% of the company's research and engineering work, up from effectively none in February, with about 30,000 agents running research and engineering tasks under human supervision as of August. The company framed the disclosure around the oversight measures it uses to keep automated agents in check as they take on more of the work that builds its next model.

Source: Spectrum News

Policy & Business

Trump plans an "AI Force" and an AI czar. President Trump said he intends to create an "AI Force" — loosely modeled on the Space Force — and to appoint an AI czar, while rejecting recent industry calls to deliberately slow the pace of frontier AI development. The move runs counter to a "pacing" consensus that several AI leaders had floated earlier in the month.

Source: The Washington Post · NBC News

Nvidia-backed Nscale files for a U.S. IPO. AI data-center company Nscale filed publicly for a U.S. initial public offering, applying to list on the NYSE under the ticker "NSCL." The London-based firm told investors it has about $51 billion in contracted revenue but reported a net loss of roughly $1.02 billion on $140.6 million in revenue for the first half of 2026. Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, and its board includes Sheryl Sandberg and Nick Clegg.

Source: CNBC · Nscale
