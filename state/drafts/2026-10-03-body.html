The frontier race intensified this week: Google unveiled its new top-end model, OpenAI shipped a cheaper reasoning model while shelving its flagship, and Anthropic refreshed Sonnet and set a timeline to go public. Here are the seven developments worth knowing from the last few days.

Models & Releases

Google introduces Gemini 4 Argon. On October 1, Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model aimed at sustained-reasoning work across software engineering, enterprise tasks and cybersecurity. Google raised the single-pass output limit to one million tokens (from 64,000) and set introductory API pricing at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output. Access is limited at launch: Argon is rolling out first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind Program, citing security risk, with no public release date given. Google · CNBC

OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Sol and cancels GPT-6.1 Astra. At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol, a mid-tier reasoning model it says approaches the performance of its GPT-6 Astra flagship at roughly a fifth of Astra's token prices ($2 per million input, $10 per million output, with cached input cut to $0.10). OpenAI also confirmed it scrapped the planned GPT-6.1 Astra release; the Wall Street Journal reported the model regressed on alignment tests and on staying within user-authorized scope. OpenAI · The Next Web

Anthropic updates Claude Sonnet 5.5. Released September 28, Sonnet 5.5 is a faster, more efficient revision of Anthropic's mid-tier model. Anthropic says it runs more than 30% faster and costs up to 30% less for most work, partly by making fewer tool calls, at the same $2/$10-per-million pricing as Sonnet 5. It is available via the Claude API, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. VentureBeat

Ant Group's InclusionAI announces Ling-3.1-flash. Announced September 30, Ling-3.1-flash is a 560-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with about 25 billion parameters active per token, targeting agent, search and office-software tasks. It is available through a free two-week API trial (context capped at 256,000 tokens, versus a planned one million); InclusionAI says it will enable the larger window and release the weights as open source after the trial, which have not yet been published. TechNode

Tavus previews Griffin-Lite, a real-time video model. On October 1, Tavus introduced Griffin-Lite, a preview of what it calls a "Human Interaction Model" — a full-duplex, video-to-video system that perceives and responds in real time during a face-to-face conversation. Tavus reports that 48% of participants in a short video-call study thought they were speaking with a person, and that the system placed first on Nvidia's full-duplex video benchmark. It is available only as a research preview to selected testers while the company works on safety and disclosure. Tavus · Crypto Briefing

Industry & Business

Anthropic sets a timeline for a November IPO. Bloomberg reported October 1 that Anthropic is targeting a public-market debut as soon as mid-to-late November, with an October 14 meeting for select institutional investors at its San Francisco headquarters and formal marketing potentially starting the week of November 9. Reports put the targeted valuation as high as roughly $2 trillion. Anthropic's prospectus flags U.S. government-related risks, citing a February order directing federal agencies to stop using its models and a Defense Department supply-chain designation. Bloomberg · Yahoo Finance

Armadin raises $255.5M for AI offensive security. Kevin Mandia's security startup Armadin announced a $255.5 million Series B co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Accel, at a valuation above $2.5 billion, bringing total funding to about $445 million. The company builds an AI-native platform that runs autonomous agents to probe an organization's attack surface and chain weaknesses into validated kill chains so defenders can close them. Armadin emerged from stealth in March 2026. Help Net Security · Tech Funding News
