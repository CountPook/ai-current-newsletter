Today in AI Current: capable AI agents keep moving onto local hardware, the economics of AI compute shift again, and the field's largest labs signal a rare turn toward cooperation. Here's what happened over the past 48 hours.

Products & Agents

Perplexity brings its on-device agent to Windows. On September 14, Perplexity released Portable Computer, the local version of its Perplexity Computer agent, for Windows PCs. The agent plans and carries out multi-step tasks entirely on the local machine and ships with Qwen 3.8 27B, an open model Perplexity post-trained and optimized for Nvidia RTX hardware. It requires a GeForce RTX or RTX PRO GPU with at least 24GB of VRAM and is available to Pro and Max subscribers through the Windows app, expanding availability that had previously been limited to Linux. (NVIDIA, Tom's Hardware)

Infrastructure & Research

Nvidia details Vera Rubin efficiency gains. At the AI Infra Summit (September 15–17 in Santa Clara), Nvidia laid out efficiency figures for its next-generation Vera Rubin NVL72 system, reporting up to 30x higher throughput per megawatt than its current GB300 NVL72 on the DeepSeek V4 Pro model. In separate pre-release testing, analyst firm SemiAnalysis measured a 2.1x gain in tokens per second per megawatt over the GB300 at 100 tokens per second, widening at higher output rates. SemiAnalysis noted the tests used unreleased hardware with vendor assistance, so the results are directional and workload-specific rather than independent production numbers. (NVIDIA, SemiAnalysis)

Policy & Safety

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind coordinate on safety. OpenAI said it has been in talks with rivals Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety for several weeks, according to a September 15 Bloomberg report. OpenAI policy chief Chris Lehane said the companies do not believe they need an antitrust waiver to work together on safety issues, and that OpenAI would support bipartisan legislation aimed at mitigating catastrophic AI risks. The disclosure follows weekend remarks in which several leading lab heads called for slowing frontier AI development. (Bloomberg, CNBC, TechCrunch)

Funding & Deals

Profound raises $180M for AI-search visibility. Profound, a two-year-old New York company whose platform helps brands track and improve how they appear in AI-generated answers — a practice known as answer-engine optimization — raised a $180 million Series D co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins. The round values the company at $1.8 billion, roughly double its February valuation, and brings total funding above $335 million. Profound says it serves more than 1,000 enterprise customers, including Comcast, Estée Lauder, and Walmart. (TechCrunch, Bloomberg)

Tandem Health lands $100M for clinical AI. Stockholm-based Tandem Health raised €86.5 million (about $100 million) in a Series B led by the Scaleup Europe Fund, which is managed by EQT. The company began with AI-assisted medical documentation and is expanding into a broader clinical operating system that prepares patient summaries before appointments, assists clinicians during consultations, and automates documentation and coding afterward. (Tech Startups)
